Plane spotting at Malta International Airport

A practical workflow for planning an aircraft photograph in Malta while respecting airport security, private property and everyone using the surrounding roads and public spaces.

Published by ThreeOne Aviation · Updated 17 August 2026

Before you go

Start with the movement you want to photograph, then work backwards. Check the airport's current arrivals and departures, the weather and the likely runway direction. Schedules change and a listed flight is never a guarantee, so keep enough flexibility for delays, substitutions and runway changes.

  • Use the official airport flight information as the starting point for scheduled movements.
  • Check wind, cloud and visibility shortly before leaving rather than relying on an old forecast.
  • Pack water, sun protection and a lens cloth; glare, dust and heat are recurring practical issues in Malta.
  • Charge batteries and leave room on the memory card before an unusual movement is due.

Malta International Airport has two runway alignments. Runway 13–31 is the primary runway, while 05–23 provides additional operational flexibility. The direction in use depends on operational and weather factors; never position yourself on the assumption that the previous movement predicts the next one.

Choose for runway direction and light

A legally accessible viewpoint is only useful when the aircraft path and the sun work together. Look at where the sun will be at the expected arrival time and choose a public position that keeps the aircraft lit from the front or side. Midday summer light can produce severe heat haze over long stretches of ground, so a closer side-on composition often keeps more detail than a distant shot through hot air.

For Runway 31 movements, think beyond the moment of touchdown. An approach photograph, a side profile and a wider environmental frame tell different stories. Leave space in front of the nose, watch poles and wires in the background, and decide in advance whether your priority is a clean identification image or a photograph that shows the aircraft in Malta's landscape.

The terminal observation deck has historically provided a designated view over the airfield. Access and opening arrangements can change, so verify them with Malta International Airport before making a special trip. The airport states that photography or filming on airport grounds requires a permit; consult its current photography guidance when that applies to your plans.

Useful starting camera settings

There is no single aircraft setting. These are starting points to test against your lens, the light and the effect you want:

Jets in flight

Start around 1/1000 second, continuous autofocus and burst shooting. Raise ISO only as far as needed to hold the shutter speed and a useful aperture.

Propeller aircraft

Try 1/160–1/320 second for visible propeller movement. Pan smoothly and shoot a short sequence; the lower keeper rate is worth the more natural result.

Ground movement

Use a smaller aperture when you want both aircraft and setting readable. Watch the shutter speed as light falls, especially with a long lens.

After sunset

Support the camera where permitted, expose for highlights and review sharpness at full size. Bright apron lights can fool automatic exposure.

Use continuous autofocus with an aircraft-tracking mode if it is reliable on your camera. If it hunts against a blank sky, use a smaller focus area and hold it over a high-contrast part of the fuselage. Review the first ordinary movement before the aircraft you came for: check highlight clipping, shutter speed and heat-haze softness while there is still time to adjust.

Safety and spotting etiquette

A photograph is never worth entering a restricted area, stopping unsafely or creating concern around critical infrastructure.

  • Remain in places lawfully open to the public and obey signs, barriers and instructions from airport or police personnel.
  • Do not block a road, gate, pavement, driveway, emergency access point or a landowner's access.
  • Do not climb fences, place equipment through a perimeter or leave bags unattended.
  • Never use a laser or bright light near an aircraft.
  • Ask before entering private land and leave the area as you found it.
  • Keep conversations and tripods out of other people's way, especially at shared viewpoints.
  • Follow current Transport Malta requirements for drones; conventional plane spotting does not make drone flight near an airport acceptable.

Turn the photograph into a useful record

Soon after the session, record the registration, aircraft type, airline or operator, date, approximate location, runway direction and whether the aircraft was arriving or departing. Preserve the original file and its metadata. Add a short note about an unusual livery, diversion, lighting condition or technique while the detail is still fresh.
